How the calculations work

Featherpeak Fury · Every formula the app uses, in full

Nothing in the app is a black box. This page lists the exact model behind each number so you can judge how much weight to give it.

Difficulty score (0–100)

Five factors, each capped by its own weight, are added together. Because every factor has a ceiling, one extreme value cannot alone push a trip into the top band.

FactorWeightReaches its ceiling at
Elevation gain30%2000 m
Distance25%30 km
Carried load20%30% of your body weight
Moving time15%10 hours
Terrain10%Snow and ice

Terrain factors: grass and path 0.30, sand and gravel 0.60, rocky and technical 0.85, snow and ice 1.00.

Bands: 0–25 easy, 26–50 moderate, 51–75 hard, 76–100 very hard.

If a factor is missing, it is excluded and the score is scaled across the factors that remain. The screen shows what share of the model was evaluated, so a half-filled trip is never presented as a confident result.

Peak profile

The curve places your elevation gain along your distance as a single main climb with minor undulations, then classifies each segment:

  • Uphill — gaining more than 25 m per kilometre.
  • Hard section — gaining more than 110 m per kilometre.
  • Downhill — losing more than 25 m per kilometre.
  • Flat — everything in between.

Rougher terrain produces more undulation. The shape is generated from the trip's own identifier, so it never changes between visits. It is a conditional illustration of your numbers, not surveyed map data.

Feather Load

Total carried mass = declared pack weight + every itemised piece of gear × quantity + water at 1 kg per litre.

Compared against your body weight from the profile screen:

  • Up to 15% — comfortable.
  • 15–20% — the upper end of the usual day-hiking range.
  • 20–25% — above the comfortable range.
  • Over 25% — flagged as heavy.

A single category above 40% of the pack is highlighted as unbalanced. That is information, not a verdict.

EggPack (food)

One Egg Unit = one conventional meal portion.

  • Base: one unit per person per 4 hours of moving time, rounded up, minimum one unit per person.
  • Intensity: light ×0.90, moderate ×1.00, high ×1.20, very high ×1.35.
  • Reserve: one extra unit per person once moving time reaches 6 hours.
  • Weight: a unit is treated as roughly 150 g when carried over into the load.

Moving time means active hours, excluding overnight stops. That is why the field is labelled moving time rather than trip duration.

CoinBudget

Only what you enter is counted. Totals are summed per category, the share of each category is shown as a percentage, and the per-person figure divides the total by the group size. No prices are fetched and no currency is converted — the symbol is a display preference.

Fury Zones

Threshold checks that run against the plan. Each one states the figure that triggered it:

  • Steep climb — 80 m of gain per kilometre draws attention, 120 m or more is high.
  • Large elevation gain — 1000 m draws attention, 1500 m or more is high.
  • Heavy load — above 20% of body weight, or high above 25%.
  • Long day — 8 hours of moving time draws attention, 10 hours or more is high.
  • Terrain — snow and ice draws attention, rocky ground is a notice.
  • Low water — below roughly 0.4 litres per person per hour of moving time.
  • Food below the estimate — packed food weight under 60% of the EggPack recommendation.
  • Unbalanced pack — one category over 40% of the load.
  • Solo on a demanding route — group of one on a hard or very hard trip.

The overall level rises with the number of serious flags rather than with any single one: one is worth a look, two need attention, three or more make it a demanding plan.

Units

Everything is stored in metric — kilometres, metres, kilograms, litres — and converted only for display. Switching units re-renders your saved trips without rewriting them, so no precision is lost either way.

What the app deliberately does not do

  • No GPS tracking, no live navigation, no maps.
  • No weather forecasts. The weather illustrations are decorative labels, not a data feed.
  • No accounts, no cloud sync, no sharing to a service.
  • No medical, fitness or calorie advice.

Featherpeak Fury is a preparation aid. Conditions on the ground, local information and your own judgement always take priority.
